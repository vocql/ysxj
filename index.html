Mod Team Setup Guide

A step-by-step path for standing up a moderation team across Twitch, YouTube and Discord from scratch — or auditing one that already exists.

  1. Define what a mod is actually responsible for

    Before adding anyone to a role, write down the job in one sentence per platform. For VOQCL that's: keep chat readable, enforce the rules consistently, and escalate anything that isn't a clear judgment call. Everything downstream — who you pick, what tools they get, how you train them — follows from this.

  2. Pick people, not fans

    The best mods are usually the most active calm chatters, not the most active chatters. Look for people who already de-escalate, already know the rules without being told, and don't need to be the center of attention when they act.

    • Watch chat for a few weeks before offering anyone a role
    • Prefer people across different time zones so coverage isn't one block of hours
    • Never hand out mod as a reward for donations, gifted subs, or friendship alone

  3. Set up roles on each platform

    Grant the actual platform permissions (Twitch mod badge, YouTube channel moderator, Discord Moderator/Admin role) only after step 4's onboarding is done, not before.

    PlatformHow access is grantedReversible?
    Twitch/mod username in chat, or Creator Dashboard → Community → RolesYes — /unmod
    YouTubeLive chat "..." menu → Add moderator, or Studio → Community → ModeratorsYes — remove from moderator list
    DiscordAssign the Moderator/Admin role via Server Settings → Roles, or a bot commandYes — remove the role

  4. Onboard before they touch a single tool

    Every new mod reads the same three docs before their first shift: this hub's Code of Conduct, the Escalation Ladder, and the command reference for whichever platform they're modding. Pair new mods with a veteran for their first week.

  5. Standardize logging

    Every action that isn't trivial (timeout, ban, warn, ticket close on a report) gets logged somewhere staff can review it. VOQCL uses a Discord #mod-log channel fed automatically by the bot for Discord actions, and a shared log for Twitch/YouTube actions since neither platform logs moderator actions long-term on its own.

  6. Review, don't set-and-forget

    Revisit the mod list every month or two. Remove inactive mods gracefully (inactivity isn't a punishment), rotate coverage, and update the rules doc whenever a new situation exposes a gap.

Why this order matters

Handing out permissions before onboarding is the single most common mistake — it turns "who should we trust" into "who already has the badge," and badges are much harder to walk back than they are to grant.

Escalation Ladder

The same five-rung ladder applies whether the platform is Twitch, YouTube or Discord. The tool changes; the logic doesn't.

Rung 1 Verbal / chat correction

A public or DM reminder of the rule. No formal action taken, nothing logged beyond a mental note. Reserved for first-time, low-severity, clearly-not-malicious situations (off-topic spam, mild language in a non-family stream, etc).

Rung 2 Warning

A formal, logged warning. On Discord this is a tracked /warn. On Twitch/YouTube it's a DM or a clearly directed chat message plus a note in the shared log. Two warnings inside a rolling 30 days escalate automatically to Rung 3.

Rung 3 Timeout / temporary restriction

Twitch /timeout, YouTube's built-in timeout, or a Discord timed timeout/mute. Duration scales with severity — start at 10 minutes, not a day, unless the behavior is severe on its own merits.

Rung 4 Kick / channel ban (single platform)

Removes access to one platform's chat or server. This is the first rung that requires two staff members to agree, or a lead mod's sign-off, except in obvious cases (slurs, threats, illegal content).

Rung 5 Cross-platform ban

Reserved for severe or repeat offenses — hate speech, harassment campaigns, doxxing, raiding, predatory behavior toward minors. Always logged with evidence, always reviewed by leadership, and always communicated to the rest of the mod team so it's enforced everywhere VOQCL has a presence.

Skipping rungs

The ladder is a default, not a hard rule. Severe first offenses go straight to Rung 4 or 5 — nobody should get a "verbal warning" for a slur or a threat. Use judgment, then log the reasoning so the next mod understands why the ladder was skipped.

When in doubt

If you're not sure which rung applies, act at the lower rung and flag it to a lead mod immediately. It's much easier to escalate a Rung 2 to a Rung 4 five minutes later than it is to walk back a ban.

Moderator Code of Conduct

What VOQCL expects from anyone holding a mod badge, on any platform.

Consistency over mood

The rules apply the same way whether you're having a great day or a rough one, and whether it's a stranger or a friend breaking them. If a rule only gets enforced sometimes, it isn't a rule.

De-escalate first

Most chat problems are solved with a calm, clear message before any tool gets used. Reach for the ladder's lowest effective rung, not the most satisfying one.

No unilateral bans on gray areas

Clear-cut violations (slurs, threats, spam bots, explicit content) can be actioned solo. Anything that requires judgment calls gets a second opinion when one is available.

Log everything non-trivial

If another mod would need context to understand why an action happened, it needs to be written down — not just remembered.

No power-tripping

Mod tools exist to protect the community, not to settle personal disagreements. Using them against someone because of an unrelated dispute is grounds for removal from the team.

Confidentiality

Reports, DMs, and staff-channel discussions about specific users stay inside the mod team unless leadership decides otherwise. Don't screenshot mod chat into public channels.

Grounds for immediate removal from the team

  • Abusing mod tools against a personal enemy or for a joke
  • Leaking private mod-team discussions or report details
  • Enforcing rules inconsistently based on favoritism
  • Ban evasion facilitation or looking the other way for friends
  • Any conduct that would get a regular member banned

Roles & Permissions

Twitch has four practical chat tiers. Each one stacks on top of the last — a moderator has everything a VIP has, plus enforcement tools.

RoleGranted byCan do
Viewer Default Chat, use emotes they own/unlock, react. Subject to slow/follower/sub-only mode.
VIP /vip username Everything a viewer can, plus bypasses slow mode and follower/sub-only mode. No enforcement powers.
Moderator /mod username Full chat enforcement — timeouts, bans, message deletion, chat mode toggles, AutoMod queue review.

Editors are not mods

A channel "Editor" (Creator Dashboard access) is a completely separate permission from chat moderator. Editors can touch stream info, clips and uploads — they have zero chat moderation power unless separately modded.

VOQCL's Twitch tier expectations

  • VIP — long-time community members who help set tone but aren't ready for (or don't want) enforcement responsibility.
  • Moderator — trained staff who've completed onboarding per the Mod Team Setup Guide.

Twitch

AutoMod & Chat Settings

AutoMod catches messages before they post; Shield Mode is the panic button for coordinated attacks. Both live under Creator Dashboard → Settings → Moderation.

AutoMod categories

AutoMod scores incoming messages against categories and holds anything over your configured threshold in a moderator approval queue instead of posting it instantly.

CategoryCatches
DiscriminationSlurs and identity-based hate content.
Sexual contentExplicit or highly suggestive sexual language.
AggressionThreats and hostile/bullying language.
ProfanityGeneral swearing.
BullyingTargeted insults and harassment phrasing.

Each category is set on a slider from off to strict. VOQCL runs Discrimination and Aggression on strict, Sexual content on strict, and Profanity on a lighter setting to keep chat casual.

Blocked terms & permitted terms

Beyond AutoMod's built-in categories, broadcasters and mods can maintain a manual block-list (exact phrases that are always held/blocked) and a permit-list (terms AutoMod would otherwise flag but that are fine for your community — inside jokes, channel-specific slang, etc).

The AutoMod queue

  1. Held messages appear in the moderator view's AutoMod queue in real time.
  2. Mods approve (message posts normally) or deny (message is discarded, sender is not automatically actioned).
  3. Repeated denials for the same user are a strong signal to move to a manual timeout.

Shield Mode

Shield Mode is a pre-configured bundle of protections (raised AutoMod levels, follower/sub-only chat, emote-only mode) you can trigger instantly with /shieldmode or one click during a hate raid or targeted harassment wave, then turn off once things settle.

Configuration tip

Set up Shield Mode's protections before you need them. Trying to configure it mid-raid is too late — the whole point is a one-click response.

Twitch Starter Guide

First shift as a Twitch mod for VOQCL? Read this top to bottom before you touch a single command.

  1. Get familiar with the moderator view

    Open chat in the dedicated moderator view (the shield icon) rather than the regular embedded chat — it surfaces the AutoMod queue, user cards, and quick-action buttons you'll actually use.

  2. Know the panic buttons cold

    /clear and /shieldmode should be muscle memory before you ever need them. If chat suddenly fills with slurs or explicit spam, clear first, ban/timeout second, ask questions never.

  3. Default to timeout, not ban

    A 10-minute timeout is reversible and low-stakes; a ban isn't. Reserve permanent bans for clear Rung 4/5 situations per the Escalation Ladder.

  4. Use the AutoMod queue actively

    Don't let it pile up — held messages that sit too long frustrate legitimate chatters. Check it every few minutes during an active stream.

  5. Log anything you'd need to explain later

    Bans, multi-message deletions, and anything involving harassment reports get a line in the shared mod log — username, action, reason, timestamp.

  6. Coordinate with co-mods live

    VOQCL mods stay in a private Discord voice/text channel during stream so bans and raids get a second set of eyes when time allows.

Cheat sheet

Keep Native Chat Commands open in a pinned tab during your first few shifts until the syntax is second nature.

Good to know

YouTube moderator status is per-channel and is not the same as being a channel "Manager" or "Editor" in YouTube Studio — those are separate permission grants under channel settings that control upload/analytics access, not live chat.

Adding a moderator

  1. During a live stream, open the live chat panel
  2. Find the user's message, click the three-dot menu next to it
  3. Select Add moderator

Alternatively: YouTube Studio → Settings → Community → Moderators → add by channel URL, which works outside of an active stream.

The "held for review" queue

YouTube automatically holds messages it suspects are spam or abusive from users without an established history in your chat. Moderators can approve or remove each one from the "Held for review" tab of the live chat panel — functionally similar to Twitch's AutoMod queue.

No native permanent chat ban

YouTube's "Hide user" is the closest thing to a chat ban and only applies going forward on your channel — it won't retroactively purge every past message, and determined ban-evaders can return under a new Google account. Combine it with blocked words and quick moderator response.

YouTube

Blocked Words & Filters

Studio → Settings → Community → Automated filters is where YouTube's word-level filtering lives.

Blocked words list

Any message containing a word on this list is automatically held for moderator review instead of posting immediately. VOQCL keeps slurs, common ban-evasion phrases, and known spam-link fragments on this list.

Approved users

Users who've chatted a lot without issue can be marked so their messages skip the held-for-review queue — useful for trusted regulars and VIP-equivalent viewers.

Block links

A single toggle to block all links from chat except from moderators — VOQCL keeps this on by default during raids/large streams and relaxes it during smaller, calmer streams.

Caps and symbol spam

YouTube's automatic spam detection handles excessive caps and symbol spam without manual configuration, but it isn't perfect — the held-for-review queue is the backstop.

Building a blocked-words list that doesn't overreach

  • Start narrow — slurs and obvious spam patterns first, expand as real problems appear
  • Avoid blocking common words that appear in normal conversation (over-blocking frustrates real viewers more than it stops bad actors)
  • Review the held-for-review queue weekly to catch words worth adding
  • Keep the list in sync loosely with the Twitch AutoMod terms so cross-platform viewers see consistent enforcement

YouTube

Strikes & Community Guidelines

This is about the channel's standing with YouTube itself, not individual chat users — every mod should understand it because chat moderation choices can affect it.

How the strike system works

YouTube issues Community Guidelines strikes against a channel (not a specific video) when content violates policy. Strikes escalate in consequence and expire after 90 days if no further strikes are issued.

StrikeConsequence
WarningFirst-time, low-severity violation — no upload restriction, but it's a signal to review what triggered it.
1st strikeRestricted from uploading, live streaming, and some other features for 1 week.
2nd strikeSame restrictions for 2 weeks. Strikes within a 90-day window compound.
3rd strikeChannel is terminated.

Why chat matters here

Live chat content, including what your moderators do or fail to remove, can factor into channel-level reviews — a live stream with unmoderated hate speech, harassment, or explicit content in chat is a real risk to the channel's standing, not just a bad viewer experience.

Zero-tolerance items

Threats, hate speech, sexual content involving minors, and content facilitating dangerous acts should be removed and reported the moment a mod sees them — don't wait to see if it escalates.

YouTube Starter Guide

First shift moderating a VOQCL YouTube live stream — here's the rundown.

  1. Confirm your moderator status is active

    Check for the wrench icon next to your name in live chat — that's YouTube's visual confirmation you're recognized as a moderator for that stream.

  2. Watch the held-for-review queue, not just live chat

    A lot of the real moderation work happens in the queue, not in the visible chat stream. Check it every few minutes.

  3. Use "Hide user" early on clear bad actors

    Since there's no timeout-then-ban ladder as granular as Twitch's, don't hesitate to hide a user once behavior is clearly not going to improve with a warning.

  4. Escalate anything that could be a Guidelines issue

    If chat content looks like it could put the channel at risk (see Strikes & Guidelines), remove it immediately and flag it to a lead — don't wait for it to become a pattern.

  5. Log actions the same way you would on Twitch

    Same shared mod log, same expectations — username, action, reason, timestamp.

Roles & Permissions

Discord permissions stack per-role and can be overridden per-channel. VOQCL's server uses a five-tier structure, enforced both by native Discord permissions and by the bot's own permission checks.

RoleNative permissionsBot command access
Member Send messages, react, join voice, use Join-to-Create. Public commands only — leveling, tickets, giveaways entry, suggestions.
Helper Manage messages in select channels. Ticket claiming, suggestion review — no punitive commands.
Moderator Kick, Timeout Members, Manage Messages, View Audit Log. Full moderation command set — warn, timeout, kick, purge.
Admin Ban Members, Manage Roles (below their own), Manage Channels. Everything Moderator has, plus ban, staff application review, ALT detection config.
Owner / Founder Administrator (all permissions). Everything, plus bot configuration commands.
Discord's permission model, briefly

Role permissions are additive by default and evaluated top-to-bottom by role position, but an explicit channel-level deny always beats a role-level allow. When something "isn't working" for a specific channel, check channel permission overrides before assuming the role is misconfigured.

Native Discord Tools

Before any bot command, Discord itself ships real moderation infrastructure. VOQCL's bot layers on top of this, not instead of it.

AutoMod

Server Settings → Safety Setup → AutoMod lets you build rule sets that block or flag messages the moment they're sent, no bot required.

Rule typeWhat it does

Each rule can trigger a block, a timeout, an alert to a log channel, or all three at once.

Server Settings → Audit Log records every administrative action — role changes, channel edits, bans, kicks — with who did it and when, whether it was done manually or via bot. This is the ground truth when reconstructing what happened in a dispute; the bot's own #mod-log is a faster-to-read summary layered on top, not a replacement.

ALT Detection Engine

Leonida Bot flags likely alt/ban-evasion accounts on join using a weighted scoring model. It never auto-bans — it surfaces a score and lets a human decide.

Policy: no auto-ban

The engine's job is to reduce how much manual digging a mod has to do, not to make the call itself. Account age and join patterns produce false positives constantly — a high score is a prompt to review, never an automatic action.

Signals scored on join

SignalWeightWhy it matters
Account ageHighAccounts created in the hours/days before joining are disproportionately likely to be evasion attempts.
Default/no avatarMediumWeak signal alone, but stacks meaningfully with other flags.
Username pattern matchMediumRandom-string or sequential-number usernames typical of bulk-created accounts.
Rejoin velocityHighMultiple joins from a similar footprint in a short window.
Shared invite sourceLow–MediumJoining via the same invite link recently used by a banned account.

Commands

CommandWhoWhat it does
/altcheck userModRuns the scoring model on demand and shows the full signal breakdown, not just a final number.
/altconfig threshold valueAdminSets the score at which a join gets flagged in the review channel.
/altlogModShows recent flagged joins and how each was resolved (cleared, kicked, banned).

Reviewing a flag

  1. Read the full signal breakdown, not just the score — a new account with a normal avatar and no rejoin history is a different situation than one hitting every flag.
  2. Check message history (if any) once the account has been in the server a moment.
  3. When in doubt, do nothing and observe — most flagged accounts are legitimate new users.
  4. Only act (kick/ban) when there's a real pattern beyond the automated score, or clear confirmation of ban evasion.

Discord Starter Guide

First shift as VOQCL Discord staff — the essentials before your first ticket claim or timeout.

  1. Read #mod-log history

    Scroll back through recent cases before your first action — it tells you what's actually been enforced lately and how, better than any written policy can.

  2. Default to /warn or /timeout, escalate from there

    Same ladder logic as every other platform — see the Escalation Ladder.

  3. Claim tickets promptly, close them cleanly

    An unclaimed ticket sitting for hours is the most common complaint about support systems — claim it, or hand it off explicitly if you can't help.

  4. Treat ALT flags as prompts, not verdicts

    Review the breakdown per ALT Detection Engine before acting on any flagged join.

  5. Use /history before escalating

    Check whether this is a first offense or a pattern before deciding how hard to come down — the case history is there specifically so you don't have to guess.

Common Scenarios

Situations that come up repeatedly, with the actual play — not just theory — for each platform. Read these before you need them.

Hostile Raid Bot / Link Spam Wave Ban Evasion Targeted Harassment Doxxing Attempt Self-Harm Mention In Chat Minor Disclosure / Safety Concern Streamer-vs-Streamer Drama Handling a Report That Turns Out False Copyrighted Links / DMCA Risk
🟥 Hostile raid / hate raid

Signs: a sudden wave of new/near-identical accounts posting slurs, spam, or copy-pasted hate messages in a short window.

  • Twitch: trigger /shieldmode immediately, then /clear. Ban the obvious accounts; don't waste time on individual timeouts mid-raid.
  • YouTube: enable Members-only or Subscribers-only mode from Live Control Room, mass-hide the worst offenders, remove pinned/held messages.
  • Discord: run /lockdown on public channels if the raid is hitting the server directly, review the ALT Detection log for the accounts involved.

Afterward: log the incident with account names/IDs where possible (helps identify patterns for next time), and debrief with the team — was it linked to a specific clip, video, or controversy?

🟪 Bot / link spam wave

Signs: repeated near-identical messages pushing a link (crypto scams, "free followers" bots, adult content bots) from multiple accounts.

  • Twitch: enable /uniquechat and tighten AutoMod's link filtering; ban confirmed bot accounts rather than just timing them out — bots don't learn.
  • YouTube: toggle "block links" in chat settings; add the scam domain fragment to the blocked words list.
  • Discord: use /purge filtered to the offending accounts, and check AutoMod's spam link filter is enabled server-wide.

These waves are usually automated and not personal — the fastest resolution is almost always tightening chat mode settings first, then cleaning up after.

🟦 Ban evasion

Signs: a new account behaving identically to a previously banned user — same phrasing, same targets, same timing patterns.

  • Don't assume — check for genuinely matching behavior, not just a coincidence in name style.
  • Discord: run /altcheck for a scored breakdown before acting.
  • Ban evasion is treated as an aggravating factor on top of whatever the original offense was — see the Escalation Ladder.
  • Log the connection between the old and new account clearly for future reference.
🟥 Targeted harassment of a specific viewer or the streamer

Signs: repeated hostile messages directed at one person, pile-ons, coordinated mockery.

  • Act on the instigators directly rather than only removing individual messages — the pattern is the violation, not just each message in isolation.
  • Check in privately with the target if it's a community member, not just the streamer — they may not want to keep chatting and that's fine.
  • Escalate straight to timeout/ban rather than a warning if it's clearly coordinated (multiple accounts, timed together).
🟪 Doxxing attempt (real name, address, phone, etc. posted in chat)

This is always Rung 5. No warning, no timeout-first — remove the content immediately and ban.

  • Delete/clear the message the second it's spotted — don't leave it up "for evidence," screenshot it privately in the mod log instead.
  • Ban across every platform VOQCL controls, and inform the affected person privately.
  • Report to the platform (Twitch/YouTube/Discord Trust & Safety) using the templates in Report Templates — this is exactly the kind of case platforms want escalated to them directly.
🟦 Self-harm mention in chat

Signs: a viewer expresses distress or references self-harm, whether seriously or as a dark joke.

  • Take it seriously regardless of tone — don't assume it's "just chat culture."
  • Don't moderate it away with a timeout as a first response; that reads as punishment for being vulnerable.
  • A calm, brief, kind acknowledgment goes further than silence. If you don't feel equipped to respond, tag a co-mod quietly rather than ignoring it.
  • This is a wellbeing situation first and a moderation situation second — the goal is making sure the person is okay, not enforcing a rule.
🟥 A viewer discloses they're a minor in a way that raises a safety concern

Signs: an adult in chat directing private/romantic attention at someone who's identified themselves as underage, or a minor sharing concerning personal details publicly.

  • Intervene immediately and directly — don't wait to see if it escalates further.
  • Remove the adult from chat/server; this is not a warning-first situation.
  • Don't publicly interrogate the minor for details — keep any follow-up private, brief, and non-judgmental.
  • Escalate to platform Trust & Safety reporting immediately — this category is exactly what those teams exist for.
🟪 Drama between VOQCL and another creator spills into chat

Signs: viewers bringing up an unrelated dispute, trying to bait a reaction, or brigading in support of "their side."

  • Keep the stream's chat focused on the stream — redirect or remove off-topic drama-baiting regardless of which "side" it favors.
  • Mods stay neutral publicly even if they have personal opinions on the drama.
  • Don't let a single loud thread dominate the enforcement queue — apply the same rules as any other off-topic spam.
🟦 A report turns out to be false or exaggerated

Signs: after investigation, the reported user didn't actually do what was described.

  • Close the ticket/report without action, and note why in the log — it protects the reported user's history from an unfair mark.
  • If false reports become a pattern from one person, that's its own conversation — don't ignore repeated bad-faith reporting.
  • Never take action "just in case" on a report that doesn't hold up under a quick check.
🟥 Copyrighted content links posted in chat

Signs: links to pirated streams, ROM/game file downloads, or ripped copyrighted media.

  • Remove the message and link immediately — this is a real risk to the channel, not just a chat-quality issue.
  • A single instance from an otherwise fine community member can be a warning; repeated posting is a ban regardless of intent.
  • Don't click unfamiliar links to "check" them — treat any suspicious file-sharing link as a removal-on-sight situation.

FAQ

Questions that come up constantly during onboarding and shifts.

Do I need to be a mod on every platform to help out?

No. Plenty of VOQCL staff only mod Discord, or only Twitch. Roles are granted per-platform based on where you're active and trusted, not as a package deal.

What's the difference between a timeout and a ban?

A timeout is temporary and reversible by design — it mutes someone for a set window without removing them permanently. A ban is meant to be lasting. Default to timeout unless the offense clearly warrants a ban per the Escalation Ladder.

Can I mod solo, or do I need someone else to sign off?

Clear-cut violations (slurs, threats, explicit spam) can be actioned alone on any platform. Judgment calls — especially anything Rung 4 or above — should get a second opinion when one is reasonably available.

What if I make a mistake — timeout the wrong person, delete the wrong message?

Fix it immediately (untimeout, restore context if possible), log what happened and why, and let a lead mod know. Mistakes happen; not disclosing them is the actual problem.

Where do I report something I'm not sure how to handle?

The private staff channel on Discord. If it's happening live and urgent, ping whoever's online immediately rather than waiting for a reply.

Does the ALT detection engine ever auto-ban anyone?

No — by design it never takes action on its own. It only surfaces a score and signal breakdown for a human to review. See ALT Detection Engine.

Why isn't my new Discord slash command showing up?

Newly registered commands need the guild-specific command tree synced after being copied from the global tree — this can take a moment, or may need a manual re-sync if the bot restarted mid-deploy.

Is this hub only for staff?

The command references are public and anyone's welcome to browse them. The internal policy pages (Code of Conduct enforcement notes, escalation specifics) are written for staff but aren't secret.
